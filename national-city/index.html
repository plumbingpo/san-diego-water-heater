Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in National City

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement.
