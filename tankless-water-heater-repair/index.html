A tankless water heater keeps no reserve of hot water. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is efficient and space-saving, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In San Diego, CA, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

San Diego Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in San Diego, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so a typical tankless repair is done in one trip. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Tankless repair is a poor fit for DIY for two reasons: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. A mistake with either one is not a small mistake.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Scale is removed with a pump through the service valves rather than vinegar poured in and left to chance. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also keeps you in line with the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
