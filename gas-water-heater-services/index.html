No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Across San Diego, CA, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

San Diego Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every San Diego call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Once we know the cause, the repair is usually simple. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight

Why Professional Gas Water Heater Services Matters

Start with the hazards. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Our technicians approach gas heaters with instruments rather than assumptions. They measure thermocouple millivolts, read gas pressure at the valve, test draft at the hood, check for CO around the appliance, and leak-test every joint they touch. That process fixes the actual fault the first time and leaves your home safer than before. We also give you an honest assessment of the heater itself. A tank past about ten or twelve years with a failing valve or a leak is usually a replacement, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a repair.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
